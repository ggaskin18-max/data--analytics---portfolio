### Project 01 | Nova Retail Group — Sales & Profitability Analysis

**Business Question: How can a company generate strong sales revenue while experiencing profitability challenges?**

Imagine a retail company reporting millions in sales, yet some of its products consistently generate losses.

Is the problem weak demand, excessive discounting, poor product profitability or differences in regional performance?

This was the central challenge I investigated in my Nova Retail Group project.

**Dataset:** 9,994 historical retail transactions

**Tools:** Google Sheets, MySQL, Power BI and DAX

#### My Analytical Approach

I began by cleaning and validating the dataset in Google Sheets, checking for missing values and confirming the accuracy of financial calculations.

I then used MySQL to investigate sales and profitability across product categories, sub-categories, customer segments, regions and discount levels.

Finally, I developed a two-page interactive Power BI dashboard to communicate the findings through financial KPIs and business-focused visualisations.
![alt text](image.png)
![alt text](image-1.png)

#### Key Findings

**1. Strong revenue did not always translate into strong profitability.**

The business generated approximately **$2.30 million in revenue**, but only **$286,397 in profit**, representing a **12.47% profit margin**.

Furniture generated approximately $742,000 in sales but achieved a profit margin of only 2.49%.

This demonstrated why revenue alone can provide an incomplete picture of financial performance.

**2. Heavy discounting was associated with substantial losses.**

Transactions without discounts achieved a profit margin of approximately 29.51%, while several heavily discounted product groups experienced significant losses.

The analysis highlighted the importance of evaluating discounting strategies against profitability rather than sales volume alone.

**3. Regional performance revealed opportunities for improvement.**

The West achieved a profit margin of approximately 14.94%, compared with 7.92% in the Central region.

These differences suggested that regional profitability deserved closer investigation.

#### Business Recommendations

- Review discount policies for consistently unprofitable product groups.
- Investigate loss-making Furniture sub-categories.
- Monitor profit margins alongside revenue in management reporting.
- Examine regional differences to identify opportunities for improved profitability.
- Use interactive dashboards to support ongoing performance reviews.

**What This Project Demonstrates**

My ability to combine spreadsheet validation, SQL analysis and Power BI reporting to investigate a business problem and translate findings into practical recommendations.

### Project 02 | Vertex Healthcare Group — Employee Attrition Analysis

**Business Question: Where should an organisation focus its employee retention efforts?**

Employee turnover can create recruitment costs, operational disruption and workforce planning challenges.

However, identifying the areas most affected by attrition requires more than simply counting the number of employees who leave.

This project investigated workforce attrition patterns across departments, job roles and employee characteristics.

**Dataset:** 1,470 employee records

**Tools:** MySQL, Python, pandas, Matplotlib and Excel

#### My Analytical Approach

I used SQL to explore employee attrition patterns, grouping records by department, job role and demographic characteristics.

I then extended the analysis using Python and pandas to validate the findings, calculate group-specific attrition rates and visualise important patterns using Matplotlib.

One of the most important aspects of this project was distinguishing between **employee departure counts and attrition rates**.

#### Key Findings

**1. The department with the most departures did not have the highest attrition rate.**

Research & Development recorded 133 employee departures, the largest number across the departments.

However, Sales recorded the highest departmental attrition rate at approximately 20.63%, compared with 13.84% in Research & Development.

This finding demonstrated why workforce size must be considered when comparing employee turnover.

**2. Certain job roles showed greater retention challenges.**

Laboratory Technicians recorded 62 departures, while Sales Executives recorded 57.

Further analysis of job-specific attrition rates helped distinguish roles with large numbers of departures from roles experiencing proportionally greater turnover.

**3. Workforce patterns require careful interpretation.**

The analysis explored differences across employee age groups, departments and roles.

Rather than assuming these patterns established the causes of attrition, I treated them as evidence for further investigation.

#### Business Recommendations

- Prioritise retention reviews in departments and roles with elevated attrition rates.
- Use exit interviews and employee feedback to investigate possible contributing factors.
- Review workload, career progression and employee development opportunities.
- Monitor both departure counts and attrition rates.
- Develop consistent workforce reporting to identify emerging retention risks.

**What This Project Demonstrates**

My ability to use SQL and Python together, validate analytical results, distinguish between different performance measures and communicate findings without overstating what the data proves.

**Project resources:** SQL analysis, Python visualisations, findings and detailed documentation are available in the Vertex Healthcare Group project folder.

## My Analytical Process

Every dataset tells a story, but understanding that story requires a structured approach.

### 01. Understand the Business Problem

Before analysing data, I identify the question the analysis needs to answer.

What decision will the findings support? Which metrics matter? What would make the results useful to the business?

### 02. Examine and Validate the Data

I assess the dataset for missing values, inconsistencies, duplicates, incorrect data types and other potential quality issues.

I also consider whether the available data is sufficiently complete to answer the business question.

### 03. Explore and Investigate

Using SQL, Excel or Python, I explore patterns, relationships, trends and unusual results.

I compare metrics carefully and consider whether alternative calculations or groupings could change the interpretation.

### 04. Visualise and Communicate

I use Power BI and Python visualisations to make complex findings easier to interpret.

My focus is on selecting visuals that communicate useful information rather than simply making dashboards visually appealing.

### 05. Recommend and Reflect

I translate analytical findings into practical recommendations while acknowledging uncertainty, data limitations and areas requiring further investigation.

**For me, the most valuable part of data analytics is connecting technical analysis with meaningful business decisions.**

## What Makes My Approach Different?

I don't believe completing an analysis simply means producing a chart or calculating a metric.

A useful analysis should also consider:

**Is the data reliable?**  
Poor-quality data can lead to misleading conclusions.

**Are we measuring the right thing?**  
A high number of employee departures doesn't automatically mean a department has the highest attrition rate.

**What is the business implication?**  
Strong revenue means little if profitability is consistently weak.

**What are the limitations?**  
Patterns and correlations should not automatically be interpreted as proof of causation.

**What should happen next?**  
Analytical findings become more valuable when they help guide further investigation or practical decisions.

These principles influence how I approach each project in this portfolio.
